EmbeddingGemma 2 — first executed surgical-video retrieval pilot

Model: google/embeddinggemma-2, official weights hash verified.
Device: mps; dtype: float32; vector size: 768.
All 60 vectors finite and unit-normalized.
Raw source: public UCL pituitary phantom video. 12-second windows, 8-second stride; video only, no annotation labels or overlay text. No patient footage used.
Index + initial query time: 270.3 seconds (excludes installation/downloads).

Prespecified evaluation: 4 instrument-state queries.
Hit@1: 1/4. Hit@3: 2/4.
Blunt dissector: relevant first result.
No instrument visible: relevant results in top three, first result missed.
Kerrisons and pituitary rongeurs: no relevant result in top three.
Relevance means target visible for >=50% of annotated seconds in a window.
These four queries on one video are a pilot, not generalization evidence.

UI verification: preset query returned three ranked windows; clicking the first jumped to 16 seconds with video readyState 4. Additional typed query returned different intervals (152, 88, 120 seconds), 0.21-second query time. Scores are similarity, not confidence. Tracking remains annotation-seeded optical flow.

Conclusion: local multimodal search operates end-to-end, but specialized instrument retrieval is unreliable on this recording. Do not promote it as accurate surgical recognition. Next useful scope is surgeon-selected review questions and independent-case retrieval evaluation, with a task-matched surgical encoder comparator.
